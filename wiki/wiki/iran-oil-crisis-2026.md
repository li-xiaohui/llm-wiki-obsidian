# Iran Oil Crisis (2026)

**Summary**: The US-Israel war against Iran and the resulting closure of the Strait of Hormuz have caused a surge in global energy prices, forming a key backdrop to geopolitical and market events in 2026.

## Context

A US-Israel war against Iran is underway in 2026, resulting in high geopolitical tension and economic disruption. The conflict has led to rising global energy prices and the disruption of commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. (source: What did Donald Trump achieve in talks with Xi Jinping?.md)

## Strait of Hormuz

The vital waterway has been closed or disrupted to commercial shipping. Trump sought Chinese help in pressuring Iran to open it. However, Trump also made remarks on May 15 that the US "does not need the Strait of Hormuz to open" — a comment that soured market sentiment. (source: What did Donald Trump achieve in talks with Xi Jinping?.md, South Korea's world-beating stock rally stumbles as global funds sell.md)

## Diplomatic Efforts

At the [[trump-xi-summit-may-2026]], Trump and Xi reportedly had "similar views" on how the Iran war should end, but Beijing offered no public commitment to pressure Iran. Trump is considering lifting sanctions on Chinese purchasers of Iranian oil as a potential concession to Beijing. (source: What did Donald Trump achieve in talks with Xi Jinping?.md)

## Market Impact

Rising oil prices from the Iran crisis have contributed to:
- Higher bond yields globally
- Reduced risk appetite in Asian markets
- A contributing factor in the [[south-korea-stock-market]] sell-off on May 15
- Broader uncertainty weighing on investor sentiment
