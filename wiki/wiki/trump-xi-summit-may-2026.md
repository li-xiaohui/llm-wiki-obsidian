# Trump-Xi Summit (May 2026)

**Summary**: A two-day summit between Trump and Xi Jinping in Beijing in May 2026 yielded no major breakthroughs but cemented a fragile stability between the US and China, with Xi introducing the concept of "new constructive strategic stability."

**Sources**: What did Donald Trump achieve in talks with Xi Jinping?.md, Nvidia's Future in China Remains Unclear After Trump-Xi Summit.md

**Last updated**: 2026-05-16

---

## Overview

Trump visited Beijing on May 14-15, 2026 for talks spanning the Temple of Heaven, the Great Hall of the People, and the gardens of Zhongnanhai. The summit produced no clear breakthroughs on foreign policy or economic fissures and fell short of delivering major business deals. (source: What did Donald Trump achieve in talks with Xi Jinping?.md)

## Key Outcomes

**What was achieved:**
- Cemented fragile stability and mutual non-aggression at a time of high geopolitical tension
- Xi introduced the concept of "new constructive strategic stability" — keeping competition within proper limits to ensure predictable peace
- Chinese state media reported Trump agreed to the concept
- Trump said Boeing secured an order for 200 planes with GE engines (no details given)

**What was not achieved:**
- No formal announcements of new contracts for US companies
- No regulatory changes in Beijing
- No Chinese commitment to pressure Iran on the Strait of Hormuz
- No progress on Nvidia chip sales to China
- Chip export controls were not discussed

(source: What did Donald Trump achieve in talks with Xi Jinping?.md, Nvidia's Future in China Remains Unclear After Trump-Xi Summit.md)

## Taiwan

Xi warned that "mishandling" of Taiwan could result in conflict. Trump said on Air Force One he had not yet decided whether to press ahead with a planned $14 billion arms sale to Taiwan. He dismissed worries about conflict: "I think we'll be fine." (source: What did Donald Trump achieve in talks with Xi Jinping?.md)

## Iran

Trump sought Chinese help pressuring Iran to open the Strait of Hormuz. While the two leaders had "similar views" on how the Iran war should end, Beijing offered no public commitment. Trump also said he was considering lifting sanctions on Chinese purchasers of Iranian oil. (source: What did Donald Trump achieve in talks with Xi Jinping?.md)

## Xi's Strategic Framing

Xi raised the Thucydides Trap concept and suggested it was a pressing danger. His "new constructive strategic stability" formulation was interpreted by analysts as:

- An attempt to impose "guardrails" on the US (Ja Ian Chong, NUS)
- A setup to criticise Washington for being unreliable (Chong)
- Related to cold-war US-Soviet "strategic stability" but with greater trade engagement (Zhu Feng, Nanjing University)
- A form of "leader-led detente but still with deep red lines on Taiwan" (Kevin Rudd)

(source: What did Donald Trump achieve in talks with Xi Jinping?.md)

## Shift in US-China Negotiating Dynamics

Andrew Gilholm (Control Risks) noted that the nature of US-China negotiations has shifted:

- Previously: US demands + pressure, China manages with concessions
- Now: China handles the US "more by deterrence than concessions"
- China makes concessions only in exchange for US concessions
- China proved willing to meet escalation with escalation (2025 tariff retaliation, rare earth export controls)
- This explains why a year of diplomacy has not brought a major breakthrough

(source: What did Donald Trump achieve in talks with Xi Jinping?.md)
