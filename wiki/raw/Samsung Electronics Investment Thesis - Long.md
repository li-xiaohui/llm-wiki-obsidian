# Samsung Electronics (005930.KS) — Investment Thesis: LONG

## Elevator Pitch

Samsung Electronics is the world's largest memory chipmaker and a newly minted $1 trillion company, sitting at the centre of an AI memory supercycle that its own executives say will create shortages through at least 2027. The stock sold off 8.6% on May 15, 2026 — erasing ~$100 billion in market cap — on a labour dispute and foreign fund momentum selling, not deteriorating fundamentals. Q1 2026 delivered record revenue (KRW 133.9T), record operating profit (KRW 57.2T), and a 48x surge in semiconductor profit. Samsung has qualified HBM3E with Nvidia, begun HBM4 mass production for Nvidia's Vera Rubin platform, and sold out its entire 2026 HBM supply. The selloff creates an asymmetric entry into a structural winner of AI infrastructure buildout.

Source: [GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/term/pettm/XKRX:005930), [CompaniesMarketCap](https://companiesmarketcap.com/samsung/pe-ratio/)

### Valuation Assessment

The trailing P/E of ~29x appears expensive vs. Samsung's own history (10-year median 12.33x). However, the forward P/E of ~5.9x reflects the market pricing in massive earnings growth from the AI memory cycle. If Q1 2026 earnings (KRW 57.2T OP) are sustainable or growing, the stock is cheap on a forward basis. The key question is whether the current supercycle earnings represent a new baseline or a cyclical peak.

## 5. LABOUR DISPUTE (KEY NEAR-TERM RISK)

### Union Demands

- Scrap existing bonus cap
- Allocate 15% of operating profit to worker bonuses (vs. management offer of 10%)
- Formalize bonus terms in employment contracts (permanent, not one-time)
- 7% base salary increase for all staff

### Management Position

- Offered 10% of operating profit allocated to bonuses
- Offered one-time special compensation for 2026
- Refused to commit to permanent changes in bonus calculation

### Strike Timeline

- April 23: Union rally drew 40,000 workers; caused 58% drop in foundry production and 18% drop in memory production that day
- May 12: Final negotiations collapsed
- May 13: $66 billion intraday market cap wipeout; Seoul government stepped in to calm fears
- May 15: Union indicated negotiations resume only after June 7
- **May 21: Threatened 18-day strike date** (41,000+ workers expected)

### Potential Financial Impact

- 18-day strike estimated cost: KRW 30 trillion (~$20 billion) to Samsung
- Broader Korean economy impact: could exceed KRW 40 trillion ($26.8 billion)
- Cost per day if strike proceeds: ~$700 million

Source: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/13/samsung-electronics-pares-66-billion-rout-as-seoul-moves-to-calm-strike-fears-involving-41000-workers.html), [Tom's Hardware - final talks](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/samsung-and-union-resume-talks-10-days-before-planned-18-day-chip-factory-strike), [Tom's Hardware - talks collapse](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/samsungs-last-ditch-union-talks-collapse-eight-days-before-planned-18-day-chip-factory-strike), [DigiTimes](https://www.digitimes.com/news/a20260513VL211/samsung-labor-strike-operating-profit-sk-hynix.html)

### Assessment

This is the highest-impact near-term risk. However:
- Seoul government has actively intervened (emergency meeting called)
- Samsung's previous labour actions were resolved without prolonged production impact
- The union's demands (15% vs. 10% profit share) have a negotiable middle ground
- A full 18-day shutdown of memory fabs would hurt Samsung's customers (Nvidia, hyperscalers) and create market-wide supply crunch — creating pressure on both sides to settle
- The April 23 rally's 18% memory production drop was one day; an extended strike affecting HBM production would trigger customer intervention

## 6. GEOPOLITICAL POSITIONING

### Unique Straddling Position

Samsung uniquely operates across the US-China technology bifurcation:

- **South Korea**: Primary R&D and advanced fabs (Pyeongtaek complex)
- **China (Xi'an)**: Major NAND flash production facility
- **USA (Austin, Texas)**: Advanced logic foundry
- **Vietnam**: Consumer electronics manufacturing

### Why This Matters

- Samsung supplies memory to both US hyperscalers AND Chinese firms
- Memory chips face less restrictive export controls than logic/GPU chips
- Both sides of the US-China split are building redundant AI infrastructure — doubling demand
- China's chip self-sufficiency push (Huawei, DeepSeek) focuses on logic chips, not memory
- Samsung benefits from the bifurcation rather than being hurt by it

### Evidence from Sources

- DeepSeek optimized for Huawei logic chips but still needs Samsung/SK Hynix memory
- Nvidia's H200 stalled in China, but memory components face no equivalent restriction
- US-China summit yielded no new restrictions on memory trade

## 8. BULL CASE SUMMARY

1. **AI memory supercycle with years to run**: Management warns shortages persist through 2027. DRAM prices rising 47% in 2026. HBM demand from Nvidia Vera Rubin, Google TPU7, and every major AI accelerator.

2. **Samsung catching up on HBM**: Market share recovered from 17% to 35% in one quarter. HBM3E qualified with Nvidia. HBM4 in mass production. >30% of Nvidia HBM4 supply secured. Entire 2026 HBM output sold out. 50% capacity expansion planned.

3. **Earnings trajectory is exponential**: Q1 2026 OP exceeded all of FY25. Forward P/E of ~6x implies the market doesn't believe earnings are sustainable — if they are, massive upside.

4. **Labour dispute is solvable**: Gap between 10% and 15% profit share is negotiable. Government intervention signals political will to resolve. Full strike would hurt customers and create industry-wide crisis — both sides have incentive to settle.

5. **Shareholder returns accelerating**: 82.5% treasury share cancellation, special dividend, 50% FCF return policy. Capital return is a structural tailwind.

6. **Geopolitical beneficiary**: Supplies both sides of US-China split. Memory less restricted than logic. Bifurcation doubles infrastructure demand.

## 11. THESIS KILL CONDITIONS

Exit or fundamentally re-evaluate if:

1. **Strike proceeds for >7 days with confirmed HBM production halt** — at $700M/day, this becomes material and could trigger customer diversification
2. **Q2 2026 earnings miss badly** — would signal cycle peaking earlier than expected
3. **DRAM spot prices decline for 2 consecutive months** — would indicate supply/demand balance shifting
4. **Samsung loses Nvidia HBM4 qualification** — would confirm structural competitive disadvantage
5. **Multiple hyperscalers cut AI capex guidance** — would undermine the entire demand thesis
6. **Foreign ownership falls below 45%** without fundamental catalyst — could signal structural Korea derating

Suggested approach: Initial half position now (post-crash discount), add second half on labour resolution confirmation (after Jun 7) or on any dip to Kospi 7,000 level.
